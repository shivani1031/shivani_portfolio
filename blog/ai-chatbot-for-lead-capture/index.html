Designing an AI chatbot for lead capture

An AI chatbot for lead capture should answer the visitor's question first, then offer a next step and ask only a few qualifying questions, such as what they need, their timeline and how they prefer to be contacted. Ask for consent before storing contact details, explain how they will be used, and pass each qualified lead to a person quickly, for example by WhatsApp or email, with a summary of the conversation. Measure completed handoffs and real follow-ups, not just chats started.

What a lead capture chatbot is for

Many website visitors have a question before they are ready to fill in a form. A chatbot that can answer those questions from your own content, at any hour, and then offer a sensible next step can turn some of those visits into conversations with your team. That is the job: help first, then capture interest from people who want a follow-up, and get them to a person.

The same chatbot often handles support questions too. AI chatbots for customer support covers that side; this guide focuses on the sales conversation and the handover.

A good lead capture chatbot is not a form with a chat skin. If the first message demands a name and phone number, many visitors will simply leave. Earn the details by being useful.

Answer first, then ask

Design the conversation in this order:

  1. Answer the question. Use your own pages and documents as the source, so that answers about services, process, location or availability are accurate. Retrieval from your content, rather than the model's general knowledge, keeps it honest.
  2. Offer a next step that matches the question. Someone asking about delivery areas might want a quote; someone asking about a feature might want a demonstration or a call.
  3. Qualify briefly. Ask a few questions that help your team respond well.
  4. Ask for contact details and consent together. Only once the visitor has said yes to a follow-up.
  5. Confirm what happens next. Say who will reply, how and roughly when you aim to respond, and keep that promise realistic.

Visitors who do not want a follow-up should still get a good answer and be free to leave. That goodwill is part of the point.

What to ask: qualifying questions

Each question you add makes the conversation longer, so ask only what your team will actually use. Common choices include:

  • What they need. In their own words, or picked from a short list of your services.
  • Timeline. Whether they are exploring or need something soon.
  • Scale or scope. A rough size that tells you which offering fits, phrased in terms the visitor understands.
  • Location or language, if it changes who should respond.
  • Preferred contact method. WhatsApp, email or a phone call, and a good time.

Let the model ask these conversationally, but store the answers as structured fields, not just a transcript. Asking the model for structured output, with a fixed set of fields and allowed values, makes leads easy to sort and route. Structured output from LLMs explains how. Validate the fields in code before saving, so that a malformed email or empty need is caught.

Be careful with budget questions. Some businesses find them useful and others find they put people off. Test your own wording rather than assuming.

Consent and privacy

A chatbot that collects names, emails and phone numbers is processing personal data, and the rules that apply depend on where you and your visitors are. In India, the Digital Personal Data Protection Act applies; visitors from other countries may bring other laws, such as the GDPR in the UK and EU. This guide is not legal advice, so check your obligations with a qualified adviser. The design principles below are sensible everywhere:

  • Ask clearly. Before saving contact details, say what you will do with them, for example "May we contact you on WhatsApp about this enquiry?", and record the answer and the time.
  • Link your privacy notice inside the chat, near the point where details are requested.
  • Collect the minimum. If a phone number is enough, do not ask for an address.
  • Keep marketing separate. Consent to reply to this enquiry is not consent to a newsletter. Ask separately, if at all.
  • Know where the data goes. Conversations may pass through a model provider, a hosting service and your CRM. Check each one's terms, and avoid sending more personal data to the model than it needs to answer.
  • Set retention. Decide how long transcripts and leads are kept and delete them when that period ends.
  • Tell people it is automated. Say plainly that they are talking to an AI assistant and that a person will follow up.

Handoff to a person

The handoff is where most of the value is won or lost. A captured lead that nobody sees until next week is wasted.

  • Send a summary, not a transcript. The person picking it up should see the need, the qualifying answers, the contact preference and a short summary in one glance, with the full conversation a click away.
  • Use channels your team already watches. An email to a shared inbox, a message to a team WhatsApp Business number, or a new record in your CRM. Sending a WhatsApp message from software requires the official WhatsApp Business tools and their rules, so check those before you design around it.
  • Offer a direct route. A "message us on WhatsApp" link that opens a chat with a prefilled summary lets the visitor start the conversation themselves, which is simple and keeps them in control.
  • Escalate on request. If a visitor asks for a person at any point, stop qualifying and hand over.
  • Route by answer. Different services or regions can go to different people, using the structured fields.

Outside working hours, be honest: say when someone will reply rather than implying an instant response.

Guardrails for a sales chatbot

A chatbot that talks to prospects represents your business, so limit what it may say.

  • No invented prices, discounts or promises. If a price is not in your approved content, the chatbot should offer to have someone confirm it.
  • Stay on topic. Politely decline unrelated requests rather than becoming a free general assistant.
  • Resist manipulation. Visitors may try to make it reveal its instructions or agree to things. Keep commitments out of its power entirely. LLM guardrails and prompt injection defence covers the techniques.
  • Keep a test set. A few dozen realistic conversations, including awkward ones, rerun after every change of prompt or model.

Measuring it

Count what matters to the business, not just activity. Useful measures, tracked over time, include:

  • Conversations started, and how many got a useful answer.
  • Questions it could not answer, which tell you what content to add.
  • Offers of a follow-up accepted, and handoffs completed with valid contact details.
  • Leads your team judged genuine, and how many became real conversations or customers.
  • Time from handoff to first human reply.

Review a sample of transcripts every week, especially conversations that ended abruptly. They show where the chatbot confused people, asked too much too soon or gave a poor answer. Compare against your previous contact form only with care, as visitors who chat may differ from those who would have filled in a form.

Frequently asked questions

What should a lead capture chatbot ask visitors?

Ask only what your team will use: what the visitor needs, their timeline, a rough scope, and how and when they prefer to be contacted. Answer their question first and ask for contact details only after they agree to a follow-up.

How do I get consent in a website chatbot?

Before saving contact details, explain what you will do with them, link your privacy notice and ask a clear question the visitor can accept or decline. Record the answer and the time, and keep marketing consent separate.

Can a website chatbot hand leads over to WhatsApp or email?

Yes. It can email a summary to a shared inbox, create a CRM record, or offer the visitor a WhatsApp link with a prefilled message. Sending WhatsApp messages from software requires the official WhatsApp Business tools.

How do I measure whether a lead capture chatbot is working?

Track useful answers, unanswered questions, follow-up offers accepted, completed handoffs with valid details, leads your team judged genuine and time to first human reply, and review a sample of transcripts every week.
