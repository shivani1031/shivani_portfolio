WhatsApp AI chatbot for business: a planning guide

A WhatsApp AI chatbot for business connects the official WhatsApp Business Platform to a backend that answers from your own content using retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), records leads and hands the chat to a person when needed. Meta's guidance points automated messaging to the official platform rather than a personal number or the WhatsApp Business app, and you need people's opt-in before you message them first.

Why WhatsApp, and why AI

For many Indian businesses, WhatsApp is where customers already ask questions: about prices, stock, appointments, fees, delivery and returns. Answering every message by hand works while volumes are small, but questions arrive at night, on holidays and in bursts after a promotion. An AI chatbot can answer the common questions immediately, collect the details your team needs and pass anything complex to a person.

The AI part matters because older rule-based bots only handle the exact phrases and menus they were built for. A language model can understand a question written casually, in mixed Hindi and English, or with spelling mistakes. On its own, though, a model does not know your prices or policies, and it may invent an answer. That is why the design below grounds every answer in your own content.

The official route: WhatsApp Business Platform

There are three ways businesses use WhatsApp, and only one is meant for an AI chatbot:

  • A personal WhatsApp account. Not meant for business messaging. WhatsApp's help centre says its products are not intended for bulk or automated messaging, and treats that as a breach of its terms.
  • The WhatsApp Business app. A free app for small businesses, operated by people on a phone. It has features such as quick replies and away messages, but it is not a way to connect your own AI system.
  • The WhatsApp Business Platform. Meta's programmatic route, through which your software sends and receives messages using the Cloud API. This is the route for an AI chatbot.

Avoid unofficial tools that automate a normal WhatsApp account, for example by driving WhatsApp Web. They are not an approved way to send automated messages and can get the number restricted, so check Meta's current policies. Setting up the platform involves a Meta business account, a phone number registered for the platform and a webhook that receives incoming messages. You can connect to it directly or through an official Meta business partner; check Meta's current documentation for the setup steps and pricing, as both change over time.

The 24-hour customer service window and templates

The platform has a rule that shapes every chatbot design. According to Meta's documentation, when a customer messages or calls you, a 24-hour customer service window opens, and it resets each time they message again. Inside that window you can send free-form replies, which is where the AI chatbot does its work.

Once the window closes, you can only send pre-approved message templates. So a follow-up the next week, such as an appointment reminder or an offer, must use a template that Meta has approved, sent to someone who has opted in. Plan these templates early, because the chatbot cannot simply carry on a conversation days later.

Opt-in and consent

WhatsApp's business policy says you may only contact people who have given you their number and agreed to receive messages from your business. Meta's opt-in guidance adds that the opt-in must make clear the person is agreeing to messages and must name your business. You choose how to collect it, for example a checkbox on your website, a form in your shop or an SMS, and you are responsible for following the law that applies to you.

Good practice, also from Meta's guidance:

  • Collect opt-in separately for different kinds of message, such as order updates and promotions.
  • Explain how to opt out, and honour any request to stop, whether it arrives on WhatsApp or elsewhere.
  • Keep a record of when and how each person opted in.

Separately, think about the personal data the chatbot collects. Ask only for what you need, say why you need it, and store it securely. India's data protection law applies to personal data you process, so take advice on what it means for your business.

Answering from your own content with RAG

RAG is what makes the chatbot useful and keeps it honest. Your content, such as a price list, FAQs, product catalogue, policies and opening hours, is split into short passages and indexed. When a message arrives, the backend finds the passages most relevant to the question and asks the model to answer only from them.

A few design choices make a big difference on WhatsApp:

  • Short answers. People read WhatsApp on a phone. Ask the model for a few lines, and offer a link or a document for detail.
  • Admit when it does not know. If nothing relevant is retrieved, the bot should say so and offer a person, not guess.
  • Keep prices and stock live. Facts that change daily are better read from your system at question time than from an index that is a week old.
  • Language. Test with the way your customers actually write, including Hinglish and regional languages if they use them.
  • Conversation memory. Keep the recent messages so that "what about the blue one?" makes sense.

If your business already has a website chatbot, the same retrieval backend can serve both channels. Add an AI chatbot to your website covers the website side, and what is RAG and when to use it explains the technique.

Lead capture on WhatsApp

A WhatsApp chat already gives you the customer's number, so lead capture is about the rest: what they want, when, where and their budget range. Let the chatbot answer the question first, then ask one or two short qualifying questions. Long forms inside a chat feel like an interrogation.

Save each lead with the conversation summary to wherever your team works, such as a CRM, a Google Sheet or an internal dashboard, and alert the right person. Do not quietly add someone to promotional messages because they asked a question; promotions need their own opt-in. AI chatbot for lead capture covers qualifying questions and measurement in more depth.

Handoff to a person

WhatsApp's business policy requires businesses that use automation to offer prompt, clear and direct ways to reach a person, such as transfer to a human agent in the chat, a phone number, email or a support form. Build the handoff in from the start rather than adding it later:

  • On request. If the customer asks for a person, hand over immediately, without making them repeat themselves.
  • On uncertainty. When retrieval finds nothing relevant or the question involves a complaint, refund or anything sensitive.
  • On value. A large order or a qualified lead may deserve a person's attention straight away.
  • With context. The staff member should see the conversation and a short summary, and the bot should stay quiet while they reply.

Tell the customer what happens next and when, especially outside working hours. "Our team will reply after 10 am tomorrow" is better than silence.

Guardrails

  • Keep the bot to your business topics, and decline anything else politely.
  • Never let it promise discounts, refunds or delivery dates that your systems have not confirmed.
  • Treat customer messages as untrusted input, so that someone cannot talk the bot into ignoring its instructions.
  • Log conversations, review a sample regularly and add the questions it handled badly to your test set.

Getting started

Start narrow. Pick the twenty or thirty questions customers ask most, gather the content that answers them, and launch with handoff to a person for everything else. Review the conversations for a few weeks, then widen what the bot handles. To have the retrieval and chatbot backend built on your own content, see RAG development.

Frequently asked questions

Can I put an AI chatbot on my WhatsApp Business app number?

Not on the app itself. The WhatsApp Business app is operated by people on a phone. To connect an AI chatbot, use the WhatsApp Business Platform, where your software sends and receives messages through Meta's Cloud API.

Do customers need to opt in before a WhatsApp chatbot messages them?

Yes, if you message them first. WhatsApp requires that people have given you their number and agreed to receive messages from your business. Replies to a customer who messaged you happen inside the customer service window.

What is the 24-hour window on WhatsApp?

When a customer messages you, a 24-hour customer service window opens and resets each time they message again. Inside it you can send free-form replies. After it closes, only pre-approved templates can be sent.

How does a WhatsApp chatbot answer from my own content?

It uses retrieval-augmented generation. Your price lists, FAQs and policies are indexed, the most relevant passages are found for each question, and the model is told to answer only from them, or to offer a person if nothing fits.

Can a WhatsApp AI chatbot hand over to a human?

Yes, and WhatsApp's business policy expects clear routes to a person when automation is used. Hand over on request, when the bot is unsure, or for sensitive topics, and give the staff member the conversation history.
